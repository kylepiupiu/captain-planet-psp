# Permission strategy and rights-holder outreach

**Engineering policy:** do not interpret a disclaimer, “non-commercial”, an AI-generated image, or source availability as a license to adapt *Captain Planet and the Planeteers*.

## Path A — request formal franchise permission

Identify the **current** copyright and relevant trademark owners through current company/legal records and documentary evidence. A historical copyright credit is not proof of today's licensing authority. Do not use an unverified individual or fan account as an authorized licensor.

Any permission request must specify:

1. **Adaptation** — rights to create and modify an interactive game based on original characters, story elements and recognizable visual designs.
2. **Platforms** — Sony PSP, PPSSPP emulator, screenshots, video demonstrations, documentation and community source releases.
3. **Distribution** — GitHub public repository, forks, volunteer modifications and release binaries; whether redistributing assets is permitted.
4. **Territory & term** — internet/worldwide distribution, license duration, cancellation and transition rules.
5. **Business model** — free/no-fee, no advertising or microtransactions, and whether donations or sponsorships are allowed. Do not assume free distribution is preapproved.
6. **Community contributions** — whether volunteers can create and share new adapted sprites, missions and designs; sublicensing/redistribution terms.
7. **Branding** — use of the show name and trademarks in game title, repository, icon, screenshots, Reddit posts and domain/marketing.
8. **AI tools** — whether generated or altered assets are permissible, and what approvals/artwork rules apply.
9. **Quality & approvals** — content standards, approval workflows, moral rights/credits, take-down process and required notices.

Written approval should be reviewed before publishing playable binaries or encouraging public redistribution.

### Draft permission inquiry (do not send without verifying recipient)

**Subject:** Non-commercial PSP fan-game adaptation / community development permission inquiry

Hello Rights & Licensing Team,

I am the maintainer of a small, non-commercial Sony PSP homebrew prototype inspired by *Captain Planet and the Planeteers*. The project aims to celebrate the series while developing an educational eco-adventure with a volunteer technical community.

Before public promotion or further distribution, I want to clarify the current rights ownership and whether a written license might be available for a limited, non-commercial fan-game adaptation. The proposed scope includes recognizable character names/designs and story elements, gameplay footage, a GitHub repository with community contributions, and downloadable PSP/PPSSPP builds.

I understand that a non-commercial label or attribution does not itself grant adaptation rights. I would welcome guidance on the appropriate rights holder/contact, permissible scope, required approvals, and any licensing process.

Please let me know whom I should contact or what materials would be needed for an initial review.

Kind regards,  
Kyle Zhang  
Project maintainer

## Path B — rights-independent eco-adventure

If permission is unavailable, cease using protected franchise names, recognizable designs, scenes, dialogue and adapted story arcs. Develop new characters, art, world-building and a distinct title while **retaining generic PSP gameplay engine and systems**. Commission or create original assets with documented provenance. Do not assume superficial name changes alone remove derivative-work risk.

## No-go criteria

- No clean evidence of permission for branded character/episode assets
- No rights review for historical binary packages / preview images
- Missing attribution or incompatibility for bundled third-party fonts/data
- Misleading project language such as “all assets open source”
